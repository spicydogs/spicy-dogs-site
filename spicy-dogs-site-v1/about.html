About

I didn’t learn about dogs only in classrooms.

A large part of what I know about dogs comes from living among them.

Image placeholder — Sri Lanka / free-living dogs

I’m based in Sri Lanka, where free-living dogs are part of the everyday landscape. They move through villages, beaches, streets and communities with a degree of autonomy that most companion dogs never experience.

Watching them has shaped the way I understand behaviour. I’ve seen dogs negotiate conflict, form friendships, communicate boundaries, choose distance, adapt to people, avoid situations they don’t trust and make decisions for themselves.

That kind of learning is difficult to reproduce in a controlled training environment.

Bambi & PingPong

The dogs who changed my direction.

They pushed me to look beyond simple training answers and ask better questions.

Why does this dog feel safe here but not there? Why does one dog seek closeness while another needs space? What happens when we stop expecting compliance and start paying attention to communication?

Living with them made behaviour personal. They became the propellers of the path I’m on now.

Experience

From rescue work to behaviour.

Before working professionally in behaviour, I spent around six years involved in CNVR and rescue work in Sri Lanka. Over time, I became increasingly interested not only in rescuing dogs, but in understanding what happens when we ask them to move from one world into another.

Especially when a free-living or village dog is taken into a domestic home and suddenly expected to cope with confinement, handling, leads, unfamiliar people, separation, busy environments and a completely different level of human control.

My approach

I don’t believe every difficult behaviour needs more control.

I believe behaviour is information.

My work is relationship-based, welfare-focused and centred on understanding the individual dog. The goal is not to create a perfectly obedient dog. It is to help you understand the dog you actually have — and build a life together that works for both of you.
